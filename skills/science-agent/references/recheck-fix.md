# Recheck a claim fix

Read the proposed replacement verbatim, from the revised manuscript when a location is supplied. If the draft and the correction log differ, identify both versions and keep the finding open until the reviewed wording is the wording being closed. An existing checkmark is a claim to verify.

Open the cited primary source. For each independently checkable part of the sentence, give a short exact passage (or a table cell with its headings), a stable source link and a locator such as a page, section or figure. Use as many sources as the sentence actually cites. A DOI lookup can establish identity. To establish support you need the relevant content.

For a derived value, show the source operands, units, operation and result. Check what was counted, in which population and with what denominator. Retain the precision the source and the wording allow. Identify any inference that the authors did not themselves report.

Return the exact replacement, the evidence and arithmetic for each part, and one overall verdict:

- **Supported** — the evidence warrants every part as written. The finding is eligible to close on that wording.
- **Contradicted** — at least one part disagrees with the source. Identify it and keep the finding open.
- **Insufficient evidence** — no contradiction established, but at least one part lacks support or its source could not be inspected. Name the missing evidence and keep the finding open.

Review requests return the verdict. When also asked to update a finding, record the evidence alongside the exact supported wording before marking it fixed. Any further rewrite, including one you propose yourself, needs its own check.
